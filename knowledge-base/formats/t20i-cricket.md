# Twenty20 International (T20I) Cricket

Twenty20 International cricket is the shortest and most fast-paced format of international cricket, designed to deliver a complete match in around three hours, and has become the most commercially popular format of the modern game.

## T20I Format Rules

- In T20I cricket, each team bats for a maximum of **20 overs** (120 balls) per innings.
- T20I format prioritizes aggressive batting, with fielding restrictions during a mandatory powerplay of the first 6 overs, limiting fielders outside the inner circle.
- A Super Over is used to decide matches that finish level, including in knockout stages of major tournaments.
- Matches are typically completed within roughly 3 hours, making the format highly broadcast-friendly.

## T20I Cricket Key Characteristics

- T20I cricket was first played as an international match in 2005 (Australia vs New Zealand), the format is the newest of the three recognized international formats.
- Spawned a global wave of domestic T20 leagues, most notably the Indian Premier League (IPL), which in turn transformed the economics of the sport.
- Strategy differs significantly from longer formats — teams prioritize boundary-hitting, death-bowling specialists, and aggressive fielding over the patient, attritional approach of Tests and ODIs.
- The ICC Men's and Women's T20 World Cups are the format's flagship international events, held biennially since 2022 (previously less frequently).

## Notable Achievements

- India won the 2024 Men's T20 World Cup, defeating South Africa by 7 runs in the final, with Virat Kohli named Player of the Match.
- India successfully defended the title at the 2026 Men's T20 World Cup, with Jasprit Bumrah named Player of the Match in the final.
