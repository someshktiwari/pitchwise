# First-Class Cricket

First-class cricket is the category of domestic, multi-day cricket that serves as the traditional pathway and proving ground for players before they progress to Test cricket, played at the highest non-international level within a country.

## First-Class Cricket Format Rules

- First-class matches are played over a minimum of three days, with two innings per side, similar in structure to Test cricket.
- Statistics from first-class matches (batting averages, bowling figures) are recorded separately from international statistics and are often used to judge a player's readiness for international selection.
- Domestic first-class competitions vary by country — for example, the County Championship in England, the Ranji Trophy in India, and the Sheffield Shield in Australia.

## Key Characteristics

- Considered the traditional breeding ground for Test cricketers; strong first-class performances are typically a prerequisite for international selection in most cricketing nations.
- Emphasizes the same core skills as Test cricket — patience, technique, and the ability to bowl and bat over sustained periods.
- Many countries also run List A (one-day) and domestic T20 competitions alongside their first-class structure, mirroring the three-format international system domestically.

## Notable Domestic Competitions

- **Ranji Trophy** (India) — India's premier first-class competition, running since 1934-35, named after Ranjitsinhji.
- **County Championship** (England) — the oldest domestic first-class competition in the world, dating to 1890.
- **Sheffield Shield** (Australia) — Australia's premier first-class competition, running since 1892-93.
