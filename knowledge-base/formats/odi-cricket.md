# One Day International (ODI) Cricket

One Day International cricket is the "limited-overs" format that sits between the multi-day Test format and the fast-paced T20 format, and is the format used for the sport's flagship global event, the Cricket World Cup.

## ODI Format Rules

- In ODI cricket, each team bats for a maximum of **50 overs** (300 balls) in a single innings.
- Matches are completed in a single day (occasionally with a reserve day for knockout matches).
- Players wear colored kits and the match is played with a white ball, usually under floodlights for at least part of the game.
- Fielding restrictions apply in different phases of the innings (powerplays), limiting how many fielders can be outside a defined inner circle during specific overs to encourage attacking batting.
- The team scoring more runs across their 50-over innings wins; if scores are tied, the match is a tie, or a Super Over may be used in some tournament formats to determine a winner.

## Key Characteristics

- Requires a balance of the technical patience of Test cricket and the aggression of T20 cricket.
- The Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) method is used to recalculate target scores in rain-affected matches.
- First played in 1971 (Australia vs England), the format grew rapidly in popularity through the 1970s and 1980s, driven in large part by the introduction of the Cricket World Cup in 1975.

## ODI Cricket Notable Records

- The world record for the highest individual ODI score is 264, set by Rohit Sharma.
- Virat Kohli holds the record for most ODI centuries (54), a milestone he reached during the 2023 Cricket World Cup, surpassing Sachin Tendulkar's previous record of 49.
