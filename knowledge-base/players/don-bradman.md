# Don Bradman

**Full name:** Sir Donald George Bradman
**Born:** 27 August 1908, Cootamundra, New South Wales, Australia
**Died:** 25 February 2001, Adelaide, Australia
**Role:** Batter
**Batting style:** Right-handed
**National side:** Australia (1928–1948, retired)

## Career Overview

Don Bradman is universally regarded as the greatest batter in the history of cricket. He played 52 Test matches for Australia across two decades, spanning the era before and after World War II, and captained Australia for much of his later career.

## Don Bradman's Career Statistics (career totals, retired 1948)

- **Tests:** Don Bradman scored 6,996 runs in 52 matches, batting average **99.94** — the most famous statistic in all of cricket, and by a very wide margin the highest career Test batting average of any player with a significant number of innings
- **Centuries:** 29 in Tests, including 12 double-centuries
- **Highest score:** 334 (vs England, Headingley, 1930)

## Don Bradman's Key Achievements

- Don Bradman's Test average of 99.94 is considered statistically the greatest achievement in any major sport — no other batter with a substantial career has come close
- In his final Test innings (The Oval, 1948), he was bowled for a duck; had he scored just 4 runs, his average would have been exactly 100
- Captained Australia's famous "Invincibles" side that toured England undefeated in 1948
- Knighted in 1949 for services to cricket

## Legacy

Bradman's statistical dominance remains unmatched in cricket. The gap between his average and the next-best career Test average of any prolific batter is so large that statisticians frequently cite it as the single greatest outlier in sports statistics.
