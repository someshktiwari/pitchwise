# Laws of Cricket

The Laws of Cricket are the official rules governing the sport, historically maintained by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) in London, distinct from the tournament-specific playing conditions set by the ICC for international events.

## Basic Structure of a Match

- Cricket is played between two teams of **11 players** each.
- The core objective is to score more runs than the opposing team.
- A match consists of one or more **innings**, during which one team bats (trying to score runs) while the other team bowls and fields (trying to restrict runs and dismiss batters).

## Ways a Batter Can Be Dismissed Under the Laws of Cricket

The Laws of Cricket recognize several methods of dismissal, the most common being:
- **Bowled** — the ball hits and dislodges the stumps directly from the bowler's delivery.
- **Caught** — a fielder catches the ball after the batter hits it, before it touches the ground.
- **Leg Before Wicket (LBW)** — the ball would have hit the stumps but is intercepted by the batter's body (usually the leg) instead of the bat.
- **Run Out** — a fielder breaks the stumps with the ball while the batter is outside their designated safe area (the crease) while attempting a run.
- **Stumped** — the wicketkeeper breaks the stumps while the batter is out of their crease, having missed the ball, without attempting a run.

## Scoring Runs Under the Laws of Cricket

- In cricket, runs are scored by batters running between the wickets after hitting the ball, or automatically when the ball crosses the boundary — **4 runs** if it touches the ground before crossing, **6 runs** if it clears the boundary without touching the ground (a "six").
- Extras (additional runs not credited to a batter) can be scored via wides, no-balls, byes, and leg-byes, depending on bowling or fielding errors.

## Custodianship

The Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC), based at Lord's Cricket Ground in London, has held custodianship of the Laws of Cricket since the 18th century, periodically revising them in consultation with the ICC and other cricketing bodies.
