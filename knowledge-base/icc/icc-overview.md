# International Cricket Council (ICC)

The International Cricket Council is the global governing body for the sport of cricket, responsible for organizing major international tournaments and overseeing the sport's regulations worldwide.

## ICC Overview

- The ICC was founded in **1909** as the Imperial Cricket Conference, originally including only England, Australia, and South Africa.
- Renamed the International Cricket Conference in 1965, and later the International Cricket Council in 1989, reflecting its growing global membership.
- Headquartered in **Dubai, United Arab Emirates**.

## Structure

- Membership is divided into **Full Members** (currently 12 nations with Test status) and **Associate Members** (nations developing the sport, without full Test-playing status).
- The ICC organizes the sport's major global events, including the Cricket World Cup, the T20 World Cup, and the ICC World Test Championship.
- Responsible for maintaining the official ICC Rankings for teams and players across all three formats (Test, ODI, T20I).

## ICC Responsibilities

- The ICC sets and maintains the playing regulations for international cricket, in coordination with the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC), which historically holds custodianship of the Laws of Cricket.
- Oversees anti-corruption and anti-doping regulation across international cricket.
- Coordinates the Future Tours Programme (FTP), which schedules bilateral international cricket between member nations.

## Full Member Nations

As of the mid-2020s, the 12 Full Members with Test status are: Australia, Bangladesh, England, India, Ireland, New Zealand, Pakistan, South Africa, Sri Lanka, West Indies, Zimbabwe, and Afghanistan.
