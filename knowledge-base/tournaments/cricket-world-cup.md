# ICC Cricket World Cup

The ICC Cricket World Cup is the premier international championship of One Day International (ODI) cricket, widely regarded as the sport's most prestigious tournament.

## Cricket World Cup Overview

- The Cricket World Cup was first held in **1975** in England, making it one of the oldest continuously-run global team sports tournaments.
- Held every **4 years**, with the tournament typically featuring 10-14 teams competing across a group stage followed by knockout rounds.
- Australia is the most successful team in World Cup history, having won the tournament a record number of times.

## Format

- Teams qualify through a combination of automatic qualification (based on ICC ODI rankings) and a separate qualifier tournament for associate and lower-ranked nations.
- The tournament typically runs for around 6 weeks, culminating in a single final match to determine the champion.

## Cricket World Cup Notable Editions

- **2011 World Cup** (India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh) — India won on home soil, with Sachin Tendulkar's teammates famously carrying him on their shoulders in celebration, in what was widely seen as a fitting send-off for his long-awaited World Cup title.
- **2019 World Cup** (England) — England won their first title in one of the most dramatic finals in cricket history, tying with New Zealand after both the match and a Super Over, with England winning on the boundary-count rule.
- **2023 World Cup** (India) — held in India, with Virat Kohli reaching his 50th ODI century in the semi-final against New Zealand, breaking Sachin Tendulkar's long-standing record of 49.

## Significance

The World Cup remains cricket's most widely watched event, drawing viewership numbers among the largest in world sport, particularly across South Asia.
