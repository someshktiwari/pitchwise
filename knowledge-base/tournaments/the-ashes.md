# The Ashes

The Ashes is the historic Test cricket rivalry between England and Australia, widely regarded as one of the oldest and most storied rivalries in world sport.

## The Ashes Overview

- The Ashes rivalry dates back to **1882**, when Australia beat England on English soil for the first time, prompting a satirical obituary in a British newspaper mourning "the death of English cricket," whose ashes would be "taken to Australia."
- A small terracotta urn, said to contain the ashes referenced in that obituary, has become the symbolic trophy of the series, though the physical urn itself remains housed at Lord's in London regardless of who "holds" the Ashes.
- Played as a multi-match Test series (typically 5 Tests), alternating between England and Australia roughly every two years.

## Format

- The series winner is determined by the team that wins the most Test matches; a drawn series means the team that held the Ashes previously retains them.
- Regarded by players on both sides as among the most intense and pressurized cricket they play, given the rivalry's history and public attention.

## The Ashes Notable Series

- **2005 Ashes** (England) — widely regarded as one of the greatest Test series ever played, won narrowly by England, ending a long period of Australian dominance in the rivalry.
- **2019 Ashes** (England) — featured Ben Stokes's unbeaten 135 at Headingley, considered one of the greatest individual innings in Ashes history, helping England win from a seemingly hopeless position.

## Significance

The Ashes carries a cultural weight beyond typical bilateral cricket series, with matches often played in front of sold-out crowds and generating extensive media coverage in both countries.
